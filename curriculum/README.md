# Curriculum

This folder contains all core teaching materials for the neural decoding workshop. The content is organized into three main sections: dataset materials, introductory lectures, and mini-workshops.

## Additional Content:
### Grad Student Panels
We recommend holding two separate 45min-1h long 'Grad Student Panels' during the workshop without professors present (can be good after dinner, brings an 'after-hours' energy), with two different topics. The topics we've typically settled on are 'Life in Grad School' more generally, and 'Science Communication'. We recommend having 4-5 students per panel, and starting with a couple pre-prepared questions/topics to go over.

There's a fair amount of overlap, but the first should be focused on discussing what it's like to pursue a PhD more generally, reviewing pros and cons of grad student life, how to go about grad applications, but also good to touch on more personal anecdotes (e.g. do you struggle with work life balance? how do you deal with constant feedback from advisors?). Also a good time to state some of the things that seem obvious but people might not be aware of, like how PhD's are paid to go to school, many schools have direct admit or rotation programs, or that the majority of PhD students go to industry (and in the case of engineering/data science this is often what they intended to do), etc.. Speak to your own experiences, why did you decide to do a PhD, what made you choose your advisor?

The second is more about what goes into writing a paper, presenting research, and how that differs from say, completing an assignment. What does it really mean to be academically rigorous? How do you develop new questions to pursue? I found it helpful to give examples of how working in research differs from school work (e.g. in a class, you complete an assignment, get a grade, and move on, whereas in PhD, you may present a figure, make some changes, and then your advisor asks for something completely different the next week). This panel is also helpful to get them thinking about how they will make their presentations.

### Keynote
For the keynote - we recommend reaching out to an expert in neurotech/neuroscience and have them give a career talk. Let them know who their audience is going to be.
