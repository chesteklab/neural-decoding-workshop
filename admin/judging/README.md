# Judging Materials

This folder contains all materials needed to evaluate participant projects in a fair, consistent, and constructive manner. These resources help create a competitive yet supportive environment that motivates participants while ensuring objective assessment.

## 📂 Contents

```
judging/
├── Example Scoring Rubric.docx      # Evaluation criteria and point allocation
├── Example Scoring Sheet.xlsx        # Spreadsheet for recording scores
└── README.md                        # This file
```

## 🎯 Purpose

Project competitions add excitement and motivation to the workshop while:
- Encouraging participants to apply learned techniques
- Promoting teamwork and collaboration
- Providing concrete goals and deadlines
- Creating opportunities for peer learning
- Celebrating participant achievements

## 🏆 Recommended Judging Categories

### Category 1: Scientific Merit (30-35 points)

**What to assess**:
- Is the research question clear and well-motivated?
- Is the question appropriate for the dataset chosen?
- Does the approach make sense for addressing the question?
- Do participants understand the scientific context?

**Scoring guide**:
- **Excellent (28-35)**: Clear, interesting question with appropriate methodology
- **Good (21-27)**: Reasonable question with mostly appropriate approach
- **Adequate (14-20)**: Basic question addressed, some methodological issues
- **Needs Work (0-13)**: Unclear question or inappropriate methods

**Judge should ask themselves**:
- Would this project yield publishable insights (in educational context)?
- Do participants understand *why* they're doing what they're doing?

---

### Category 2: Technical Implementation (30-35 points)

**What to assess**:
- Code quality and organization
- Correct use of techniques taught in workshop
- Appropriate data preprocessing
- Proper train/test splitting and cross-validation
- Handling of edge cases

**Scoring guide**:
- **Excellent (28-35)**: Clean, well-organized code with best practices
- **Good (21-27)**: Code works correctly with minor issues
- **Adequate (14-20)**: Code functions but with notable problems
- **Needs Work (0-13)**: Significant technical errors or misunderstandings

**Judge should ask themselves**:
- Is the code reproducible?
- Are techniques applied correctly?
- Have they avoided common pitfalls (data leakage, p-hacking, etc.)?

---

### Category 3: Results & Interpretation (20-25 points)

**What to assess**:
- Quality of visualizations
- Clarity of results presentation
- Appropriateness of statistical tests
- Correct interpretation of findings
- Acknowledgment of limitations

**Scoring guide**:
- **Excellent (20-25)**: Clear, informative figures with accurate interpretation
- **Good (15-19)**: Reasonable presentation with mostly correct interpretation
- **Adequate (10-14)**: Basic results shown, some interpretation issues
- **Needs Work (0-9)**: Poor visualization or significant misinterpretation

**Judge should ask themselves**:
- Can I understand the results from the figures alone?
- Do conclusions follow logically from results?
- Have they oversold or misinterpreted their findings?

---

### Category 4: Communication (15-20 points)

**What to assess**:
- Presentation clarity and organization
- Ability to explain technical concepts
- Response to questions
- Story flow and engagement
- Appropriate level for audience

**Scoring guide**:
- **Excellent (16-20)**: Engaging, clear presentation with strong Q&A responses
- **Good (12-15)**: Clear presentation with reasonable Q&A
- **Adequate (8-11)**: Understandable but somewhat disorganized
- **Needs Work (0-7)**: Difficult to follow or poor understanding demonstrated

**Judge should ask themselves**:
- Could someone unfamiliar with the project understand it?
- Did they tell a coherent story?
- Can they explain their choices?

---

### Category 5: Creativity & Going Beyond (10-15 points)

**What to assess**:
- Novel approaches or insights
- Attempting advanced techniques
- Clever solutions to problems
- Interesting questions asked
- Exceeding basic requirements

**Scoring guide**:
- **Excellent (12-15)**: Impressive innovation or particularly insightful analysis
- **Good (9-11)**: Some creative elements or attempts at advanced work
- **Adequate (6-8)**: Competent work meeting basic requirements
- **Needs Work (0-5)**: Minimal effort or just following templates

**Judge should ask themselves**:
- Did they do something unexpected or clever?
- Did they show initiative beyond the basics?
- Was I impressed or surprised by anything?

## 📝 Judge Instructions

### Before the Presentations

**Preparation** (1-2 days before):
1. Review the scoring rubric carefully
2. Understand the workshop content (what participants learned)
3. Familiarize yourself with the datasets used
4. Calibrate expectations appropriately (this is a short workshop, not a thesis defense)

**Calibration Meeting** (30 minutes before):
- Meet with other judges to discuss scoring philosophy
- Review rubric together
- Discuss how to handle edge cases
- Agree on time management strategy
- Assign any specialized roles

### During the Presentations

**For each presentation**:
1. **Listen actively** to entire presentation
2. **Take notes** on strengths and weaknesses in each category
3. **Ask clarifying questions** during Q&A
4. **Record scores** immediately after presentation (while fresh)
5. **Write brief comments** for feedback

**Time management**:
- Start on time
- Enforce time limits gently but firmly
- Allow adequate Q&A time
- Take breaks between sessions if needed

**Judge behavior**:
- ✅ Be engaged and encouraging
- ✅ Ask constructive questions
- ✅ Take visible notes (shows you care)
- ✅ Smile and nod when appropriate
- ❌ Don't use phone/laptop for other purposes
- ❌ Don't whisper to other judges during talks
- ❌ Don't show negative reactions

### After the Presentations

**Deliberation** (30-60 minutes):
1. **Calculate total scores** (use provided Excel sheet)
2. **Identify top projects** (typically top 3)
3. **Discuss close calls** among judges
4. **Prepare feedback** for all teams
5. **Select winners** and any special awards

**Announcing results**:
- Thank all participants for their effort
- Acknowledge that all projects had merit
- Announce winners in ascending order (3rd, 2nd, 1st)
- Briefly highlight what each winning team did well
- Mention special recognitions if applicable
- Encourage all participants to continue their work

## 👥 Team Formation Guidelines

### Team Size
**Recommended**: 2-4 people per team

**Why this size?**:
- Solo: Can be isolating and overwhelming
- Pairs: Good for peer learning, reasonable workload
- 3-4: Allows specialization, but requires coordination
- 5+: Communication overhead, free-rider problem

### Formation Strategies

**Option 1: Self-Selected Teams**
- Let participants choose their own teams
- **Pros**: Work with friends, known working styles
- **Cons**: Can exclude some individuals

**Option 2: Assigned by Organizers**
- Mix backgrounds (neuroscience + CS + engineering)
- Mix skill levels (strong coder + strong theory)
- **Pros**: Diverse teams, ensures no one left out
- **Cons**: May have personality clashes

**Option 3: Hybrid**
- Let people form teams initially
- Organizers help unmatched individuals
- Option to merge small groups
- **Pros**: Balance autonomy and inclusion

### Ensuring Good Team Dynamics

**Day 1 team formation**:
- Discuss expectations for collaboration
- Have teams create team agreement
- Encourage clear role division
- Provide framework for conflict resolution

**Mid-workshop check-ins**:
- Ask how teams are functioning
- Help resolve any conflicts
- Ensure all members contributing
- Be ready to intervene if needed

## ⚖️ Ensuring Fair Judging

### Avoiding Bias

**Blind review (if possible)**:
- Don't reveal team member identities until after scoring
- Focus on work quality, not personalities

**Diverse judge panel**:
- Mix of genders, backgrounds, career stages
- Brings different perspectives
- Reduces individual biases

**Calibration**:
- Meet before judging to align expectations
- Discuss sample projects at different quality levels
- Agree on scoring philosophy

**Multiple judges**:
- Have 2-3 judges score independently
- Average scores or discuss discrepancies
- Increases reliability

### Handling Conflicts of Interest

**If judge has prior relationship with team**:
- Judge should recuse themselves from scoring that team
- Other judges score, or average without that judge's score

**If judge suggested the project approach**:
- Should not judge that specific project
- Organize schedule so they're not present

### Accommodating Different Backgrounds

**Remember**:
- Participants have diverse starting points
- Workshop is short (days, not months)
- Goal is learning, not publication-ready work

**Calibrate expectations**:
- Grade on appropriate curve for workshop timeframe
- Value learning journey, not just final product
- Recognize clever problem-solving even if execution is rough

## 💬 Providing Feedback

### Individual Team Feedback

**Write constructive comments**:
- Start with positives ("What you did well")
- Provide specific suggestions ("Could improve by...")
- Encourage continued work ("Next steps could be...")
- Be specific, not vague

**Example good feedback**:
"Your visualization of neural trajectories was excellent - very clear and informative. The linear decoder was implemented correctly with proper cross-validation. To strengthen this work, consider trying nonlinear methods to see if performance improves, and test statistical significance of your decoding accuracy. Great job overall!"

**Example poor feedback**:
"Good job. Maybe try harder next time."

### General Feedback to All Participants

**During closing remarks**:
- Acknowledge the challenge of learning so much in short time
- Highlight common strengths across projects
- Note interesting variety of approaches
- Encourage continued exploration
- Provide resources for next steps
